## 1. Integrity is already structurally sound — with one condition

The pipeline is: engine computes → `buildRows` applies manual overrides → the sheet displays `final_value`; on submit that becomes an **immutable snapshot**, and every report reads the snapshot's stored `final_value` rather than recomputing.

So if the ceiling lives **inside the row-building step**, the sheet and the snapshot carry the same number, and every report inherits it — integrity comes for free. The condition is that the ceiling must **not** be display formatting in the sheet component. If it were, the sheet would show ১১ while reports added up the raw taka — exactly the divergence you're guarding against. One shared helper, called in exactly one place.

Also good news: economic codes have no parent/child roll-up (only a display `category`), and the sheet's subtotals and grand total are computed as `rows.reduce(sum + final_value)` ([sheet.ts:193](file:///c:/Apps/ird-budget/src/lib/budget/sheet.ts#L193)). So ceiling each row and the categories/total reconcile automatically. Just never ceiling the total on top of ceiling'd rows.

## 2. Two consequences of "ceiling in the engine" to sign off on

**a) It inflates small codes.** Today the engine's only rounding is `Math.round` on every code ([calculate.ts:478](file:///c:/Apps/ird-budget/src/lib/budget/calculate.ts#L478)). Change that to ceiling-to-thousand and a code that genuinely computes ৫০০ টাকা becomes ১,০০০ — doubled. Across a sheet that's up to ~999 taka per code. If the official form is in হাজার then that *is* the rule, but it's worth being deliberate about it.

**b) 10.2 → 11 is a big cliff.** 10.0 → 10, but 10.05 → 11. A 50-taka change in input moves the budget by 1,000. Users will hit this and be confused.

## 3. Your example contradicts "multiple of thousand"

You wrote *"User must input in multiple of thousand"* and then *"to enter 10256, then enter 10.2"*. Those are different rules — 10.2 is a multiple of **100**, not of a thousand.

And because the output is ceiling'd to a whole thousand anyway, the decimal is **thrown away**. The only thing it does is decide which thousand you land on, via the cliff above.

My recommendation: **integers only, in হাজার, at input.** Then:
- Every input is a whole thousand — matching the official form.
- The ceiling only ever matters for *derived* figures (percentages, ×months, sums).
- The cliff disappears, because there's no way to type 10.2.
- Your 3-digit cap becomes a clean "is that digits or a typo?" check.

22,256 taka is then entered as 22 — which is the honest consequence of budgeting in হাজার.

999 হাজার = ৯.৯৯ লক্ষ. That's fine for a field office per code, but a **ministry- or department-level entity** can easily need ১,২০০ হাজার for one code. A hard global cap would make a legitimate entry impossible — worse than the typo it prevents.

Better: the cap rides along with the unit as a **per-field property** (3 digits for field-office fields, higher where the entity is bigger), and the 2nd layer is **block + explain**, not truncate. Your message is right; I'd tighten the wording since ১০,০০০ is taka and the field wants হাজার:
